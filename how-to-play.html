How to Play

Off-the-Spinner Dominoes for 4 players — rules, scoring, and match structure.

Overview

Off-the-Spinner is a 4-player domino game played over 14 rounds. Each round is built around a different double domino called the spinner. Players score points based on how many pip dots remain in their hand when someone goes out. After all 14 rounds, the player with the lowest total score wins.

What You Need

  • One double-six domino set — 28 tiles numbered 0–0 through 6–6
  • Exactly 4 players
  • A flat surface to play on
  • The Boneyard app to track scores automatically
A standard double-six set has 28 tiles. With 4 players each drawing 7 tiles, every tile is dealt — there is no boneyard pile left over at the start. This is where the game (and the app) get their name.

The 14-Round Spinner Sequence

Each round is defined by a specific double domino — the spinner. The sequence goes from Double-6 down to Double-0 over 7 rounds, then back up to Double-6 over the next 7 rounds. The Boneyard app displays the spinner for the current round automatically.

The Shaker

The shaker is the player who shuffles ("shakes") all 28 tiles face-down and deals them out at the start of each round. The role rotates clockwise through all 4 players, advancing one seat each round.

Tip: Before starting a match, use the Boneyard app's setup screen to pick who shakes first. The app will then display the shaker for every subsequent round automatically.

Playing a Round — Step by Step

  1. 1

    Shuffle & Deal

    The shaker turns all 28 tiles face-down and mixes them thoroughly. Each player then draws exactly 7 tiles and stands them up so only they can see their own tiles.

    4-Doubles Reshake: After drawing, if any player finds they have 4 or more doubles in their hand, all tiles must be returned, reshaken by the shaker, and redealt. Repeat until no player holds 4 or more doubles before play begins.
  2. 2

    Find the Spinner

    The player who holds the round's spinner (the required double) places it face-up in the center of the table to start the chain. For example, in Round 1 the spinner is Double-6 — whoever holds [6|6] plays it first.

    If no one has the spinner, players take turns drawing additional tiles from any leftover tiles until the spinner is found. The player who draws it plays it immediately.

    Full Hand Play: If the spinner holder can play all 7 of their tiles in a single turn — every tile in their hand connects and can be laid down at once — they may do so immediately. The round ends on the spot: the spinner holder scores 0, and every other player scores the full pip count of every tile remaining in their hand.
  3. 3

    Play Off the Spinner

    Play moves clockwise starting from the player to the left of whoever played the spinner. On your turn you must play a tile that attaches to your end of the chain. The spinner extends in all four directions — each player builds out their own side. (All tiles were dealt at the start, so there is no boneyard to draw from during a round.)

    If you cannot play on your side
    A player is already knocked
    You may play a tile onto their chain instead of your own.
    No one is knocked
    You become knocked — your turn is skipped and the other three players build on your chain until you can re-enter, either on your own chain or a knocked player's chain.

    A player returns to normal play as soon as they have a tile that fits somewhere available.

  4. 4

    Go Out

    The round ends the moment any player plays their last tile. That player is the round winner and scores 0 points for the round.

  5. 5

    Locked Round

    A round becomes locked when every player is knocked and no one can make any play anywhere on the table. When this happens:

    The round ends immediately — there is no round winner. Every player scores the pip count of the tiles remaining in their hand.
  6. 6

    Score the Round

    Every player who did not go out counts the total number of pips (dots) on all tiles still in their hand. That pip count is their score for the round. Scores are entered into the Boneyard app and added to each player's running total.

Scoring

Lower is better. At the end of each round you count the pips remaining in your hand and record that number. The goal is to minimize your cumulative total across all 14 rounds.

Score range: Each round score can be anywhere from 0 (went out) to as high as all your remaining tiles allow. The app accepts any non-negative whole number up to 999 per round.

Winning the Match

After all 14 rounds have been played and scored, the player with the lowest cumulative score wins the match. The Boneyard app keeps a running standings board throughout the game so everyone can see the leaderboard at any time.

Strategy note: Because the spinner sequence repeats (Double-0 appears twice, and both Double-6 rounds bookend the match), high-value tiles become dangerous in later rounds. Dumping your high pips early is key.

Glossary

Spinner
The double domino that opens a round (e.g., [6|6] in Round 1). It can be extended in all four directions at the start.
Shaker
The player who shuffles and deals the tiles for that round. Rotates clockwise each round.
Pip
A single dot on a domino face. A [4|3] tile has 7 pips total.
Go out
Playing your last tile, which ends the round. Going out earns 0 points for that round.
Boneyard
In standard domino games, the boneyard is the pile of undealt tiles you draw from. In Off-the-Spinner, all tiles are dealt at the start — no boneyard remains. The game (and this app) take their name from that pile.
Knocked
A player who cannot play on their turn and has no knocked player to play off of. A knocked player's chain becomes available for other players to extend. A knocked player re-enters play as soon as they can place a tile somewhere on the table.
Locked round
A round in which every player is knocked and no tile can be played anywhere. The round ends immediately with no winner — every player scores the pips remaining in their hand.
Running total
Each player's cumulative score across all rounds played so far.
